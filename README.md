# Kenya-Gender-Rule-Compliance-and-Performance-Scorecard
An analysis of Kenya's compliance with the constitutional two-third gender rule and the performance of women leaders in the Legislature, with a focus on County Woman Representatives 
## Background
**Article 27(6)** of the Constitution of Kenya 2010, affirms that the state shall take extensive legislative and other measures - including affirmative actions and policies - designed to redress any disadvantage suffered by individuals or groups because of past discrimination. **Article 27(8)** commits to implementing the principle that not more than two-thirds of members of the elective or appointive bodies shall be of the same gender. **Article 81(b)**, reiterates this for the electoral system, legally mandating that no single gender exceeds 66.7% of any elective or appointive position. Therefore, to operationalize these principles, **Article 97(1)(b)** establishes that the National Assembly shall consist of forty seven elected Woman Representatives, from each county, to address the gender gap in the Parliament. 

While these constitutional mandates guide government arms on how to actively encourage effective gender governance, full compliance has remained inconsistent across the Executive, Legislature and Judiciary. Furthermore, since their inception in 2013, this scorecard evaluates if and how these affirmative positions have translated into effective legislative action and crisis response at both the local and national levels.  
## Research Questions
1. To what extent has Kenya complied with Article 27(8), 81(b), 97(1)(b) two-thirds gender rule since 2010, across the Executive, Legislature and Judiciary?
2. Using women in the Legislature (Woman Representatives, elected and nominated Members of Parliament and Senators) as our case study, what is the performance of Kenyan women leaders in developing existing policy reforms, supporting Kenyan citizens during gender-related crises, and what other reforms have they advocated for to protect Kenyan women, since 2013?
3. Based on the performance cited in Q2, over the last four years, is the claim that Woman Representative seats should be scrapped justified?
## Stance
While the evidence on women leaders' performance over the last four years - including during the period of political and gender-related unrest between 2023 and 2026 - lends credibility to public calls for scrapping County Woman Representative seats, this project argues that the more justified conclusion is structural reform rather than elimination; the primary defect lies in the candidate selection, political party accountability, and governance execution mechanisms, not in the constitutional existence of the seats themselves.
